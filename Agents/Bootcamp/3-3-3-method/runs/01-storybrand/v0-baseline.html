Billy's Blog — Draft Topic Calendar

April – June 2026 · 10 Weekly Posts

Ten weekly blog topics for Billy Tijerina, Head of School, written for Keystone parents. Each entry includes a headline and summary paragraph describing the angle, sources, and Keystone connection. Topics follow Billy's established pattern: an opening epigraph, a personal school observation, outside intellectual authority, community voices, and a warm closing call to action.

Post 1
Week of April 13, 2026

"Why boredom might be the most underrated gift we can give our children"

With spring break behind us and the final stretch of the school year ahead, Billy reflects on a paradox: parents and schools work hard to fill every hour of a child's day, yet research increasingly shows that unstructured time — even the uncomfortable experience of boredom — is where creativity, self-direction, and intrinsic motivation take root. He draws on a recent piece from the American Psychological Association linking childhood boredom to divergent thinking, connects it to what Keystone teachers observe when students are given open-ended project time, and encourages parents to resist the urge to over-schedule the summer months ahead.

Post 2
Week of April 20, 2026

"What our students learn when they serve the community"

As Keystone's spring service projects wrap up, Billy spotlights several student-led initiatives — from food drives to tutoring partnerships with local organizations — and makes the case that service learning isn't an add-on to academics but a core part of how Keystone forms ethical leaders. He references Keystone's pillar of community involvement and weaves in perspectives from faculty advisors and students themselves, arguing that the most lasting lessons often happen outside the classroom walls. He closes by thanking the parent volunteers who make these efforts possible.

Post 3
Week of April 27, 2026

"How we help students navigate the anxiety of 'what comes next'"

Spring is a season of transitions — seniors preparing for graduation, eighth graders moving to Upper School, families making enrollment decisions. Billy acknowledges that transitions produce real anxiety for students and parents alike, then draws on research about adolescent identity formation to explain why some discomfort is not only normal but necessary. He highlights the advising relationships and mentoring structures Keystone has built to ensure no student navigates a transition alone, and encourages parents to name the anxiety without trying to eliminate it.

Post 4
Week of May 4, 2026

"The case for teaching students to write by hand"

In a world racing toward AI-generated text and voice-to-text dictation, Billy makes a counterintuitive argument: handwriting still matters — not as nostalgia, but as cognitive science. He cites a recent study from the Norwegian University of Science and Technology showing that handwriting activates brain networks involved in memory and learning in ways that typing does not. He connects this to Keystone's approach in the Lower School and asks whether the Upper School should be doing more, inviting parents into the conversation.

Post 5
Week of May 11, 2026

"What our mothers — and the people who mother us — teach about education"

Timed for Mother's Day weekend, Billy writes a warm, reflective post about the role of maternal figures in a child's educational journey. Rather than a generic tribute, he explores the research on how a mother's (or primary caregiver's) attitude toward learning — curiosity, persistence, comfort with not knowing — transmits to children more powerfully than any curriculum. He shares a few anonymized anecdotes from Keystone families and closes with gratitude for the partnership between home and school.

Post 6
Week of May 18, 2026

"Are we preparing students for jobs that don't exist yet — and does it matter?"

Billy takes on a cliché of modern education — the claim that we must prepare students for future jobs we can't predict — and argues that the framing misses the point. A liberal arts education doesn't aim to predict the labor market; it aims to form adaptable, literate, ethical human beings who can learn anything. He references Keystone's own AI policy work alongside broader workforce data and makes the case that the "skills of the future" — critical thinking, communication, collaboration — have been the skills of every era.

Post 7
Week of May 25, 2026

"Why Memorial Day is a teaching moment, not just a long weekend"

With Memorial Day approaching, Billy reflects on the importance of civic memory and why schools have a responsibility to help students understand sacrifice, service, and the cost of the freedoms they enjoy. He draws on Keystone's history curriculum and a recent assembly or guest speaker, and gently pushes back on the cultural drift toward treating the holiday as merely the start of summer. He encourages families to have conversations with their children about what the day means.

Post 8
Week of June 1, 2026

"What I've learned from this year's graduating class"

As commencement approaches, Billy flips the script: instead of telling seniors what he hopes they've learned, he shares what they've taught him. He profiles several moments from the year — a student who changed the tone of a difficult classroom conversation, a senior project that surprised everyone, a quiet act of leadership — without naming names but with enough specificity that the community will recognize the spirit. The post doubles as a love letter to the Class of 2026 and a preview of his commencement themes.

Post 9
Week of June 8, 2026

"The real reason we make students give speeches at graduation"

Building on his earlier post about public speaking skills, Billy explains the philosophy behind Keystone's tradition of student speeches at commencement. He argues that requiring students to articulate their experience in front of a community isn't performative — it's formative. The act of organizing your thoughts, finding your voice, and standing before people who matter to you is one of the most powerful exercises in self-knowledge a young person can undertake. He connects this to Keystone's broader mission of responsible leadership.

Post 10
Week of June 15, 2026

"A letter to Keystone families as we close this chapter"

Billy's end-of-year post takes the form of a direct letter to parents. He reflects on the year's highlights and challenges honestly, thanks the community for its trust and participation, and looks ahead to summer and the fall with optimism. He reinforces the themes that have run through the year's posts — presence, character, independence, resilience — and closes with an invitation: use the summer not to get ahead, but to slow down, read together, and let your children be bored, uncomfortable, and wonderfully themselves.
