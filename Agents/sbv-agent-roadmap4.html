01 — Orientation

How this actually works

The short version, no jargon.

Think of it as an assembly line with seven stations. A folder of the client's documents goes in one end. A drafted valuation report comes out the other. Each station does exactly one job, hands its work to the next, and writes down anything it couldn't figure out.

One job each

Every agent produces one thing — a worksheet, a memo, a number, a report. Not "handles the financials." One noun.

That's deliberate. An agent with one job either does it or visibly doesn't. An agent with four jobs fails at one of them quietly.

They never decide

No agent picks a multiple, chooses a weighting, or concludes what a business is worth. Those arrive from Nancy on an input sheet, and the agent applies them.

If an agent doesn't have a number it needs, it stops and says so. It does not estimate, default, or guess.

They flag, they don't fix

When something's wrong in the source documents — a mislabeled file, a total that doesn't add up — the agent reports it and keeps going. It never quietly corrects a number.

Everything it flags lands in one file per client, so nothing gets lost between stations.

What Nancy actually does to run it. Drop the client's documents into that client's Inputs folder. Tell the first agent the business name. It asks a handful of questions with clickable answers — no typing paragraphs — and sets up the folders. Then run the agents in numbered order. Each one finishes by showing her what it made, telling her what needs her eyes, and asking whether to continue.

03 — The team

What each agent needs, and what it gives back

Every card links to that agent's real output from the Project Firefly test run — for red pen.

About the examples. Each station's real output from the Project Firefly run is attached to the email rather than published here — they carry a live client's figures, and this page is a link anyone can open. Station 01

Intake

Sets the engagement up, reads every source document before anything else touches them, and opens the flags file the whole team writes to. The only agent that knows the client's real name.

Built · not yet run end-to-end
What it needs from you
  • The business name
  • Which tier — Express, Explore, Accelerate
  • SDE or EBITDA
  • The valuation date
  • The client's documents in the Inputs folder
What you get back
  • A codename, suggested from what the business does
  • The folder structure, ready for every other agent
  • A one-page setup file every later agent reads first
  • A flags file, if it found problems
It asks with buttons, not blanks. Tier, earnings basis, codename — all clickable options rather than open questions Nancy has to compose an answer to.
On Firefly it caught
  • A file named Payroll2025.pdf whose contents actually cover January–July 2026. It read it as 2026 data and said so — it did not rename the file or reassign the year.
  • Payroll2026.pdf has pages but no readable text — a scan. Skipped and flagged.
  • A 2026 partial year present in the documents. Excluded per your instruction on the call, recorded as a decision.
Where its findings go — the flags file

Intake creates one flags page per client, and every later station adds to it. It's the single place to look to see what needs a person before the numbers can be trusted — so it lives here, with the agent that starts it.

  • Two levels only — Blocker (don't trust the output yet) and Review (output stands, but look at this)
  • Each station adds its own section; nobody overwrites anybody
  • Every item is something a person can actually resolve
Flags are not observations. "Gross margin held healthy" is an observation and belongs in the memo. "No add-backs file was supplied" is a flag. Your distinction from the call, now enforced.

Station 02

Recast Worksheet

Takes the client's P&L exactly as their bookkeeper wrote it, adds back what a buyer wouldn't inherit, and produces normalized earnings. The foundation everything else stands on.

Built · run · audited · corrected
What it needs from you
  • Profit & loss statements — two years minimum, three preferred
  • An add-backs file, if you have one
  • Replacement compensation for the departing owner, for EBITDA
  • Tax returns, if you want them cross-checked
What you get back
  • One worksheet per year — statement, add-backs, adjusted, notes
  • A multi-year comparison with percentages
  • A summary page with SDE and the weighted averages
  • All five as printable pages, plus spreadsheets
Account names are the client's own words, verbatim. No invented categories — that's what broke the August 20 demo. Anything under $1,000 in every year rolls into "All Other Expenses," per your instruction.
Caveats on Firefly
  • No add-backs file was supplied. Officer's compensation and discretionary spending aren't normalized, so SDE is understated and the value range will move up once you send the add-backs.
  • Replacement compensation wasn't supplied, so EBITDA and EBIT are blank on every year.
  • Two different accounts in the client's books are both called "Bank Service Charges" (GL 70300 and 80009), and both have money in them in 2025. Kept separate and labeled by GL code — needs a word with the bookkeeper.
  • FY2023's expense lines were $2 short of the stated total after rounding. The $2 now sits in All Other Expenses so the column adds up on screen.
Firefly example — all five sections in one scroll Sent separately by email. The examples carry a real client's figures, so they are not published on this page.
Station 03

Revenue & Customer Concentration

Ranks who the revenue actually comes from. Concentration is one of the eight drivers, and it's the one a buyer asks about first.

Built · rebuilt from source
What it needs from you
  • Income by customer, per year
  • Nothing else — it cross-checks against the recast on its own
What you get back
  • A ranked customer table with each one's share of revenue
  • Possible duplicate customers flagged, never merged silently
  • A note where the stated total doesn't match the line items
Needs your call

How many customers should the table show — top 10, top 20, everyone above 1% of revenue? The example uses the 1% threshold, which gives 12 rows. The agent's own default is top 10, which was a guess rather than a decision.

Caveats on Firefly
  • "A Customer Residential" might be a bucket, not a customer. It's a parent account holding 11.2% of revenue spread across many individual addresses, with only a few thousand dollars billed directly to it. If it's one buyer it's the biggest customer by four times. If it's where the bookkeeper files walk-in residential work, it isn't a customer at all. Not resolved — needs the bookkeeper.
  • Two accounts sharing a surname sit at 6th and 7th. If one household, the combined total becomes the largest customer on the list at 2.60%. Flagged, not merged.
  • The customer report and the recast disagree on total revenue by roughly a third of a percent — revenue not attributed to any customer account. Neither figure was adjusted to match the other.
  • 2025 only. The other file supplied covers January–June 2026, which is outside this engagement. No trend available.
  • Customer identities are masked on the example — the books name them by address or by person, and those are third parties. See decision 02 below.

Station 04

Observations

Reads the multi-year comparison and says what it means — six observations, each a plain statement followed by two to four bullets.

Built · run · corrected
What it needs from you
  • Nothing new — it reads station 02's output

What you get back
  • A six-bullet executive summary — a thirty-second read
  • Six detailed observations: revenue, margin, largest costs, big movers, expense trend, earnings
  • A data flags section at the bottom
Statement, then bullets — your format from the call. Both the short version and the detail are in the same document, so the reader picks their depth.
Caveats on Firefly

  • Every figure here understates the business until the add-backs land.

Station 05

Earnings & Value

Collapses three years of earnings into one weighted figure, then applies the multiple. This is the station that produces the range of value.

Built · run · corrected
What it needs from you
  • Nancy's input sheet: a weight per year, summing to 100
  • Low, mid, and high multiples
  • Any note on comparable transactions
What you get back
  • The weighted earnings calculation, every step shown
  • The range of value at all three multiples
  • A methodology note in Nancy's voice
Why this exists separately from station 02. The recast tells you what the business earns. This tells you what that's worth. The multiple is the entire second half of a valuation and station 02 never touches it.
Caveats on Firefly
  • Weighting was 50 / 30 / 20 across 2025 / 2024 / 2023, and the multiple range 2.0× to 3.0× — both supplied, neither chosen by the agent.
  • The range is preliminary and will rise once owner add-backs are supplied — officer's compensation alone is the single largest line on the P&L.
  • Expected SDE growth and reasonable owner salary weren't on the input sheet, so those rows are blank rather than filled with a plausible guess.

04 — Over to you

Decisions we need before the next build

Each of these is currently running on somebody's inference. That's the part worth red-penning first.

Where you've spoken, the agents follow you. Where you haven't, they follow Nancy's prior guidance, and failing that a default we chose. These are the places where a default is currently standing in for a decision — listed so you can overrule any of them in a sentence.

01

The weighting block on the recast summary

Gary

ValuTrax's summary page ends with a weighting row and weighted averages for revenue, SDE, salary, EBITDA and EBIT. We replicate it. But station 05 applies the same weighting again to produce the range of value — so the same number is computed in two places, from two different sources, days apart.

You walked through the summary page on the August 20 call and named exactly two problems with it. A missing weighting block wasn't one of them, which we read as neither approval nor objection.

  • Keep it as is — matches ValuTrax exactly, and the two figures are cross-checked against each other
  • Show the rows but leave them blank, pointing at station 05 — keeps the familiar shape, one source for the number
  • Drop it — cleanest, but the summary stops matching the report you've used for years
02

How customers get named in the concentration table

Gary

Firefly's books name customers by property address rather than by person or company — a street number and road name per row. We carry account names through verbatim everywhere else, so the workbook does too. That means the client's customers' home addresses are sitting in a working file.

The codename system protects the client's identity. It says nothing about the client's customers.

  • Leave them verbatim — internal working files, never client-facing
  • Truncate to street name — enough to tell customers apart, less exposure
  • Label them Customer 1…N with the mapping kept in the client index
03

How many customers the table should show

Gary

Currently top 10, which we picked because we had to pick something. With Firefly's largest customer at 2.5% of revenue, ten rows tells you almost nothing — a threshold like "everyone above 1%" might read better, or might produce a forty-row table on a different client.

  • Fixed count — top 10, or top 20
  • Percentage threshold — everyone above 1% of revenue
  • Whichever is shorter, with the cutoff stated on the table
04

Who writes the Recommendations section

Gary

The reference reports carry a full recommendations section — five items, each with three or four action bullets, tied back to the observations. No agent owns it. Today station 07 leaves a placeholder for Nancy.

It's arguably the most valuable page in the report for the client, and the one that most obviously demonstrates why they paid for it.

  • Nancy writes it — placeholder stays, judgment stays human
  • Extend Observations — the agent that already knows what's wrong proposes what to do
  • New station that reads the driver gaps and drafts recommendations for Nancy to edit
05

What the Express tier actually ships

Gary

Open since before this version: does Express replace the Value Builder platform report, or supplement it with SBV-branded pages? It changes what station 07 builds for that tier, and it's the cheapest product so the answer decides how much work is worth doing.

06

Two rollup details that are ours, not yours

Gary

You settled the $1,000 threshold and the "All Other Expenses" name on the August 20 call. Two details underneath it we decided ourselves, and they're worth a glance because they change what appears on the page:

  • All-or-nothing per line. A line at $1,400 one year and $600 another stays broken out in every year — so a row can't appear and vanish down the comparison.
  • We test the adjusted figure, not the statement figure. Otherwise a rent line showing $0 with a large normalization behind it would get swallowed by the rollup.

Nancy

05 — Boundaries

What the agents will not do

Not limitations to fix later. Design decisions.

Judgment
Choosing the multiple and the weighting
These are the two inputs that most determine the answer, and both come from Nancy. An agent that could pick them could produce a valuation, and then nobody could say whose opinion it was.
Accountability
The signature
Every output is a draft until a person reviews the flags file and signs. Nothing leaves the machine on the agents' say-so. The Certified tier is out of scope entirely — the CVA credential is the whole product there.
The conversation
Walking a client through it
The reports exist so Gary can sit next to an owner and take them through the process. The agents build the artifact. They don't do the meeting, and the artifact is only as good as that meeting is easy.
One structural safeguard worth naming. When an agent doesn't know something, it stops and asks rather than filling the gap. That's why the Firefly package has blank EBITDA rows and a preliminary range instead of a confident-looking number nobody chose. A visible gap is recoverable. A plausible invention that reaches a client is not.
