01: Orientation

How this actually works

The short version, no jargon. Think of it as an assembly line with stations. A folder of the client's documents goes in one end. A drafted valuation report comes out the other. Each station does exactly one job, hands its work to the next, and writes down anything it couldn't figure out.

One job each

Every agent produces one thing: a worksheet, a memo, a number, a report. Not "handles the financials." One noun. An agent with one job either does it or visibly doesn't. An agent with four jobs fails at one of them quietly.

They never decide

No agent chooses a weighting or concludes what a business is worth. Those arrive from Nancy on an input sheet, and the agent applies them. The multiple works the same way: it is typed in, with the source named, and the agent uses what it is given. The one narrow exception is that Earnings & Value may propose a multiple from comp documents in the client's folder, which a person confirms or replaces before anything finished is produced.

They flag, they don't fix

When something's wrong or missing the agent reports it and keeps going, unless the problem makes the numbers unreliable, in which case it stops and asks. It never quietly corrects a number or invents one to fill a gap. Everything it flags lands in one place per client.

What Nancy actually does to run it. Drop the client's documents into that client's Inputs folder, including the add-backs workbook. Tell the first agent the business name. It asks a handful of questions with clickable answers and sets up the folders. Then run the agents in order. Each one finishes by showing her what it made and telling her what needs her eyes.

02: The line

Eight stations, in order

Each hands off to the next. Nancy reviews at the end, and can stop at any station.

  • 01
    Intake: Sets up the client. Checks the documents make sense.
  • 02
    Recast Worksheet: Normalizes the P&L. Produces SDE and EBITDA.
  • 03
    Balance Sheet: Transferable assets against what the owner keeps, and a walk from the value to what the owner takes home after debt.
  • 04
    Revenue & Customers: Who the revenue comes from, and how concentrated.
  • 05
    Value Builder Summary: The eight drivers, written up.
  • 06
    Observations: Where the quantitative and qualitative work meet.
  • 07
    Earnings & ValueRebuilt: Weights the years, applies the multiple, runs the income method, and reconciles the approaches to one estimated value.
  • 08
    Report Narrative: Assembles everything into the report.
  • ·
    Human: Nancy reviews and signs. Judgment, edits, sign-off. The part that isn't automated and won't be.

Intake

Built and run for real

05: Boundaries

What the agents will not do. Design decisions, not limitations to fix later.

Finalizing the multiple, and the weighting
Judgment

The weighting is Nancy's. A station can propose a multiple, but a person says yes before anything finished is produced. An agent that could finalize it could produce a valuation, and then nobody could say whose opinion it was.

The industry code
Gary's call

Confirmed by a person, read by the agent. Gary is writing up how he wants codes and multiples researched, question 7, and this may change when he does.

The signature
Accountability

Nothing is client-ready until a person has read the working notes and signed. Nothing leaves the machine on the agents' say-so.

Walking a client through it
The conversation

The reports exist so Gary can sit next to an owner and take them through the process. The agents build the artifact. They don't do the meeting.

One structural safeguard worth naming. When an agent doesn't know something, it asks rather than filling the gap. On a page that means the line is left blank, with no invented figure. The question goes to Nancy in the working notes she reads before she signs. A visible gap is recoverable. A plausible invention that reaches a client is not.
