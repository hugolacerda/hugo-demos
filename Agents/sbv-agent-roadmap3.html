Foundation

The Model Every Agent Serves

Gary's mental model. If the agents get this, the rest is detail.

Every agent in this pipeline is one step in a single reasoning chain. The chain produces one answer: here is what your business is worth, here is what determines that number, and here is what you can do about it. Agents that lose sight of this model produce generic output. The model is the connective tissue.

The Two-Lever Value Model — Gary's Framework
Lever 1 — Normalized Earnings
The base the multiple acts on. Larger earnings means a larger output — and a small size premium applies on top. This is what the normalization and weighted-earnings agents work on. Growing earnings on weak value drivers leaves money on the table.
Value = Weighted Earnings × Multiple
Lever 2 — The Multiple (a buildup of risk)
The multiple is not a lookup — it is a risk construct. A less risky, more transferable business commands a higher multiple. Risk is measured by the eight Value Builder drivers. Improving those drivers de-risks the business and raises the multiple. One full multiple point is called "a turn."
Higher VB score → lower risk → higher multiple → more value
The sequencing principle: Improve the value drivers first to build a stronger foundation, then accelerate earnings growth. Growth on top of improved drivers earns incremental value at a higher multiple — both levers move simultaneously. Gary's headline selling point: a 30% growth rate over two years, averaging 15% a year, will double the value of most businesses.
The simplicity rule: One or two takeaways per deliverable, not a data dump. "The more details I get bogged down in, the less they need us." Education leads to consultation. Agents that over-reach erode the reason to hire SBV.
Value versus Price — always stated up front: Value is the pre-market directional number SBV produces. Price is what the market actually pays. These are never the same. SBV educates every client on the difference before anything else.
Methodology

SBV's In-House Valuation Approach

The manual steps the agent pipeline replaces.

SBV does not use Value Builder's own value number for client-facing work. Value Builder is the nurture, assessment, and conversation layer. Gary: "It is not in the scope of the valuation to analyze the P&L. It is in the scope to analyze their normalized earnings."

The Three-Part Valuation Process
Step 1
Normalization (Recasting)
Normalize the income statement. Interest, Depreciation, and Amortization are always added back when present — never flagged for absence. Owner add-backs on top of IDA. Output is SDE and EBITDA. SDE for Main Street and owner-operator businesses. EBITDA for larger or institutional-buyer engagements. Nancy determines which per engagement. This is a financial snapshot, not a P&L analysis.
Net Income + IDA (always) + owner add-backs = SDE
SDE − reasonable owner salary = EBITDA
Step 2
Weighted Earnings
SDE or EBITDA from each year is weighted to produce a single representative earnings figure. The weighting is professional judgment Nancy makes per engagement — no fixed formula. Standard 50/30/20 for most three-year Express/Explore runs. Fenetex certified used 30/30/20/10/10/0 across six years. Agent 07 applies whatever Nancy specifies — never its own.
Weighted Earnings = Σ (each year × its weight)
Weights must sum to 100% — Nancy sets them
Step 3
Market Method + Range of Value
DealStats comps. Nancy selects low, mid, and high multiples after reviewing comparable sold transactions. The multiple is a buildup of risk — not a lookup. Agent 07 applies what Nancy specifies. Output is a low/mid/high range of value: the headline number on the report. Primary comp database: DealStats (confirmed on Fenetex, January 2026).
Range of Value = Weighted Earnings × Multiple
(low / mid / high — Nancy sets the multiples)

Growth Forecast · Post-Observations Deliverable
Growth Forecast
Takes the multi-year normalized summary from Agent 01 and extends it forward into five revenue-growth scenarios. Margin is held flat. Operating expenses grow at half the revenue growth rate. The result demonstrates operating leverage — EBITDA margin improves as the business scales — and anchors Gary's core selling point: a 30% growth rate over two years will double the value of most businesses.
Build next — blocked on Gary's spreadsheet
Inputs
  • Multi-year normalized summary (Agent 01 Sheet 2)
  • Base year margin (from Agent 01 output)
  • Base year operating expenses (from Agent 01 output)
Exact spec (Gary, June 16)
  • Base year as column 1
  • Five scenarios: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50% revenue growth
  • Margin stays flat across all scenarios
  • Gross profit auto-calculates as revenue × margin
  • Operating expenses grow at half the revenue growth rate (10% revenue = 5% expenses)
  • EBITDA = gross profit − operating expenses
Position in report and pipeline
  • Comes after Observations (Agent 02)
  • Comes before Recommendations and Scenario Plan
  • Its own named page in the client's report — not embedded inside another section
  • Feeds Agent 08 (Scenario Planner) as an input

What the Agents Do Not Replace

Nancy
Professional judgment + NACVA sign-off
The multiple selection, the weighting decision, the earnings basis (SDE vs EBITDA), the certified report signature, and the income approach on certified engagements. Agents apply what Nancy specifies. They never choose for her.
Gary
Strategic advisory + "the velvet hammer"
The discovery call, the engagement decision, the seller relationship, the deal structure. Knowing when to step in and when to let partner brokers run it. No agent replicates that judgment or that relationship.
Both
Client delivery + the Scenario Planner conversation
Presenting the Growth Forecast, running Value Builder's Scenario Planner and Black Box live, showing what improvements do to value. This is where advisory upsells happen. AI is a draft, never the deliverable.
