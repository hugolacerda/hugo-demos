01 — Orientation

How this actually works

The short version, no jargon. Think of it as an assembly line with stations. A folder of the client's documents goes in one end. A drafted valuation report comes out the other. Each station does exactly one job, hands its work to the next, and writes down anything it couldn't figure out.

One job each

Every agent produces one thing — a worksheet, a memo, a number, a report. Not "handles the financials." One noun. That's deliberate. An agent with one job either does it or visibly doesn't. An agent with four jobs fails at one of them quietly.

They never decide

No agent chooses a weighting or concludes what a business is worth. Those arrive from Nancy on an input sheet, and the agent applies them. The one narrow exception, from v6: a station may propose a sourced starting point, shown with where it came from. A person still confirms or overrides it before it's used for anything — the agent proposes, it never finalizes. As of v8, this posture now covers two things, not one: Station 05's sourced multiple, and Station 07's proposed NAICS code.

They flag, they don't fix

When something's wrong or missing — a mislabeled file, a total that doesn't add up, a market value nobody's supplied — the agent reports it and keeps going. It never quietly corrects a number or invents one to fill a gap. Everything it flags lands in one file per client, so nothing gets lost between stations.

What Nancy actually does to run it. Drop the client's documents into that client's Inputs folder. Tell the first agent the business name. It asks a handful of questions with clickable answers — no typing paragraphs — and sets up the folders. Then run the agents in order. Each one finishes by showing her what it made, telling her what needs her eyes, and asking whether to continue.

02 — The line

Eight stations, in order

Each hands off to the next. Nancy reviews at the end — and can stop at any station.

  • 01
    Intake — Sets up the client. Checks the documents make sense.
  • 02
    Recast Worksheet — Normalizes the P&L. Produces SDE and EBITDA.
  • 03
    Balance SheetBuilt · run twice — Transferable assets vs. what the owner keeps, cash-free/debt-free, real estate and fixed assets at market value where supplied, an enterprise-value bridge to net owner equity.
  • 04
    Revenue & Customers — Who the revenue comes from, and how concentrated.
  • 05
    Value Builder Summary — The eight drivers, written up.
  • 06
    Observations — Where the quantitative and qualitative work meet.
  • 07
    Earnings & Value — Weights, sources or applies the multiple, DCF where it applies.
  • 08
    Report NarrativeReal draft produced — Assembles everything into the draft report.
  • —
    Human — Nancy reviews & signs. Judgment, edits, sign-off. The part that isn't automated and won't be.

This is the order things run, not yet the order the folders are named. Balance Sheet New since v7

Built & run Sept 10 · first live run, unreviewed

Recasts the client's balance sheet to a seller's estimated fair market value: what it's actually worth in a sale, not what the books say it cost. Bridges from Earnings & Value's range to what the owner actually walks away with, cash-free and debt-free.

What it needs from you
  • The client's balance sheet
  • An advisor or owner estimate of real estate and fixed-asset market value — never guessed if missing
  • Earnings & Value's Range of Value for this engagement, already produced by Station 07
What you get back
  • A recast table: book value, adjustment, seller's estimated market value, for every asset and liability line
  • Real estate broken out on its own, separate from other fixed assets
  • Every distinct long-term debt on its own line, never collapsed into one
  • An Enterprise Value / Net Owner Equity bridge: starting price, plus receivables minus payables, minus estimated debt payoff, plus real estate if it's part of the sale

Two treatments for fixed assets, on purpose. The going-concern price from Earnings & Value never separately prices vehicles or equipment — "we value cash flow, not vehicles." This agent's own recast does the opposite, marking them to market individually, because it's answering a different question: what does the owner walk away with in an asset sale. The two are kept deliberately separate; this agent's numbers don't feed Earnings & Value's.

Asset Method: prepared for, not built. This agent produces exactly the recast data an Asset Method valuation would need. It does not compute one. Whether Asset Method becomes a real, standard section is Decision 07 below — unresolved, though this agent existing means the technical blocker on that decision is now gone.

Caveats on Firefly, current as of Sept 10: no market-value estimate exists yet for the real estate ($372,098 book) or the fixed assets ($3,384 net of depreciation) — both flagged, neither guessed. One combined depreciation account couldn't be split between the building and the vehicles. A $71,500 property sub-account isn't clearly explained. A $101,500 shareholder loan was zeroed out as a judgment call Nancy may want to revisit.
Firefly example — recast table + Net Owner Equity bridge. Sent separately by email.

05 — Boundaries

What the agents will not do. Not limitations to fix later. Design decisions.

Finalizing the multiple, and the weighting
Judgment

The weighting is Nancy's, full stop. A station can source and propose a multiple or a NAICS code, but a person still says yes before either is used — an agent that could finalize either could produce a valuation, and then nobody could say whose opinion it was.

The signature
Accountability

Every output is a draft until a person reviews the flags file and signs. Nothing leaves the machine on the agents' say-so.

Walking a client through it
The conversation

The reports exist so Gary can sit next to an owner and take them through the process. The agents build the artifact. They don't do the meeting.

One structural safeguard worth naming. When an agent doesn't know something, it stops and asks rather than filling the gap. That's why the Firefly package has "Pending" rows on the balance sheet instead of a confident-looking number nobody chose. A visible gap is recoverable. A plausible invention that reaches a client is not.
