01: Orientation

How this actually works

The short version, no jargon. Think of it as an assembly line with stations. A folder of the client's documents goes in one end. A drafted valuation report comes out the other. Each station does exactly one job, hands its work to the next, and writes down anything it couldn't figure out.

One job each

Every agent produces one thing: a worksheet, a memo, a number, a report. Not "handles the financials." One noun. An agent with one job either does it or visibly doesn't. An agent with four jobs fails at one of them quietly.

They never decide

No agent chooses a weighting or concludes what a business is worth. Those arrive from Nancy on an input sheet, and the agent applies them. The range of multiples comes from comparable sales or from an advisor, with the source named. Inside that range the agent places the multiple by the Value Builder score, the way Gary's method does, and it proposes the industry code. In every case a person says yes before anything finished is produced.

They flag, they don't fix

When something's wrong or missing the agent reports it and keeps going, unless the problem makes the numbers unreliable, in which case it stops and asks. It never quietly corrects a number or invents one to fill a gap. Everything it flags lands in one place per client.

What Nancy actually does to run it. Drop the client's documents into that client's Inputs folder, including the add-backs template and the Industry Report. Tell the first agent the business name. It asks a handful of questions with clickable answers and sets up the folders. Then run the agents in order. Each one finishes by showing her what it made and telling her what needs her eyes. On Thicket, answering the add-back questions as they came up took a few minutes; Nancy's view was that settling add-backs up front "is going to save a lot of time."

Intake

Built · run on Firefly, Thicket (October 13) and Lotus (October 19)

Sets the engagement up, reads every source document before anything else touches them, and opens the working notes the whole team writes to. The only agent that knows the client's real name, and it will not start without it.

What it needs from you
  • Which tier, and SDE, EBITDA or both
  • The client's documents in the Inputs folder
What you get back
  • A codename, the valuation date, and the folders every other agent uses
  • A list of problems it found in the source documents, stated as fact
A statement covering only part of a year is kept out of the recast rather than passed along. Nothing is ever stretched to look like a full year. On Thicket and Lotus the valuation date was set at the end of the most recent twelve months, so the date and the market cases rest on the same results (question 2).

06: Boundaries

What the agents will not do. Design decisions, not limitations to fix later.

Finalizing the multiple, and the weighting
Judgment

The weighting is Nancy's. The range of multiples comes from comparable sales or an advisor; the agent places the multiple inside it, and a person says yes before anything finished is produced. An agent that could finalize it could produce a valuation, and then nobody could say whose opinion it was.

The industry code
A person's call

Proposed by the agent from the largest share of revenue or gross profit, confirmed by a person before the run goes on.

The signature
Accountability

Nothing is client-ready until a person has read the working notes and signed. Nothing leaves the machine on the agents' say-so.

Walking a client through it
The conversation

The reports exist so Gary can sit next to an owner and take them through the process. The agents build the artifact. They don't do the meeting.

One structural safeguard worth naming. When an agent doesn't know something, it asks rather than filling the gap. On a page that means the line is left blank, with no invented figure. The question goes to Nancy in the working notes she reads before she signs. A visible gap is recoverable. A plausible invention that reaches a client is not.
