01: Orientation

How this actually works

The short version, no jargon. Think of it as an assembly line with stations. A folder of the client's documents goes in one end. A drafted valuation report comes out the other. Each station does exactly one job, hands its work to the next, and writes down anything it couldn't figure out.

One job each

Every agent produces one thing: a worksheet, a memo, a number, a report. Not "handles the financials." One noun. That's deliberate. An agent with one job either does it or visibly doesn't. An agent with four jobs fails at one of them quietly.

They never decide

No agent chooses a weighting or concludes what a business is worth. Those arrive from Nancy on an input sheet, and the agent applies them. The multiple works the same way and that is the normal path: it is typed in, with the source named, and the agent uses what it is given. The one narrow exception is that Earnings & Value may propose a multiple sourced from comp documents in the client's folder, which a person then confirms or overrides before anything finished is produced. The agent proposes, it never finalizes. That remains the only thing any agent proposes. The industry code proposal from v8 is gone at Gary's direction.

They flag, they don't fix

When something's wrong or missing (a mislabeled file, a total that doesn't add up, a market value nobody's supplied) the agent reports it and keeps going. It never quietly corrects a number or invents one to fill a gap. Everything it flags lands in one place per client, so nothing gets lost between stations.

What Nancy actually does to run it. Drop the client's documents into that client's Inputs folder, including the add-backs workbook. Tell the first agent the business name. It asks a handful of questions with clickable answers, no typing paragraphs, and sets up the folders. Then run the agents in order. Each one finishes by showing her what it made, telling her what needs her eyes, and asking whether to continue.

02: The line

Eight stations, in order

Each hands off to the next. Nancy reviews at the end, and can stop at any station.

  • 01
    Intake: Sets up the client. Checks the documents make sense.
  • 02
    Recast WorksheetNew design: Normalizes the P&L. Produces SDE and EBITDA.
  • 03
    Balance Sheet: Transferable assets against what the owner keeps, cash-free and debt-free, real estate and fixed assets at market value where supplied, and a walk from the published value to what the owner takes home after debt.
  • 04
    Revenue & Customers: Who the revenue comes from, and how concentrated.
  • 05
    Value Builder Summary: The eight drivers, written up.
  • 06
    Observations: Where the quantitative and qualitative work meet.
  • 07
    Earnings & Value: Weights the years, applies the multiple, runs the income method, and reconciles the three approaches to one estimated value.
  • 08
    Report Narrative: Assembles everything into the report.
  • ·
    Human: Nancy reviews and signs. Judgment, edits, sign-off. The part that isn't automated and won't be.

This is the order things run, not the folder names. The rename is still Hugo's call on timing. Until then, the station cards below follow the running order above.

Balance Sheet

Built · last run September 16, 2026 · re-runs once Earnings & Value's current figure is in

Recasts the client's balance sheet to a seller's estimated fair market value: what it's actually worth in a sale, not what the books say it cost. It then walks from the value Earnings & Value published down to what the owner actually takes home, cash-free and debt-free.

What it needs from you
  • The client's balance sheet
  • An advisor or owner estimate of real estate and fixed-asset market value, never guessed if missing
  • Earnings & Value's Range of Value for this engagement
What you get back
  • A recast table: book value, adjustment, seller's estimated market value, for every asset and liability line
  • Real estate broken out on its own, separate from other fixed assets
  • Every distinct long-term debt on its own line, never collapsed into one
  • A proceeds walk: start from the published value, add receivables less payables, add other assets less other liabilities, subtract the estimated debt payoff, add real estate if it's part of the sale, and finish at what the owner keeps

Two treatments for fixed assets, on purpose. The going-concern price from Earnings & Value never separately prices vehicles or equipment: we value cash flow, not vehicles. This agent's own recast does the opposite, marking them to market individually, because it's answering a different question: what does the owner walk away with in an asset sale. The two are kept deliberately separate.

The asset value is built. The condition Gary set was to look at how Peer Comps does it first, and that review is done. It confirmed Nancy's objection rather than overriding it: Peer Comps says in its own words that it is simply allocating which assets and liabilities are included in the sale, rather than valuing the company's assets. So the station produces its figure the same way, and the reconciliation carries it at whatever confidence Nancy gives it, usually zero. A method at zero confidence still prints, because the goodwill line is the estimated value less this number.

The heading on the proceeds walk is not settled. It currently uses the term Gary used in August, and the report rules forbid that term elsewhere. Question 8 below. Naming only; the arithmetic is unaffected either way.
Caveats on Firefly, unchanged: no market-value estimate yet for the real estate or the fixed assets, both flagged, neither guessed. One combined depreciation account couldn't be split between the building and the vehicles. A property sub-account isn't clearly explained. A shareholder loan was zeroed out as a judgment call Nancy may want to revisit. Whether the real estate is in the sale at all has not been confirmed, and the walk currently shows it included because that is the template's default step.
Firefly example: recast table plus the proceeds walk. Sent separately by email.

Earnings & Value

Rebuilt September 18 · last run September 22, 2026, on a typed-in multiple

Collapses several years of earnings into one weighted figure, applies the multiple, runs the income method, and reconciles the approaches into a single estimated value. This is the station that answers what the business is worth.

What it needs from you
  • Nancy's input sheet: a weight per year, summing to 100
  • The low, middle and high multiple, with a source name, and the number of sales behind it where she has it
  • For the income method: the discount rate components, and the revenue and margin assumptions per year
  • A confidence level per approach, for the reconciliation
What you get back
  • The weighted earnings calculation, every step shown
  • The Range of Value at all three multiples
  • The income method, with the discount rate shown as a stack rather than a single number
  • A reconciliation table carrying all three approaches to one estimated value, plus the goodwill line
The three weightings, as Gary laid them out
  • Across the years. One set of weights, applied once.
  • Across revenue, SDE and EBITDA. What gets weighted is the value each one indicates, never the multiples themselves, and the same year weights feed all three.
  • Across asset, income and market. The one that was missing entirely. This is what turns two answers into one.
The multiple is typed in, and that is the normal path. Nancy or Amanda enters the three figures with a source name and the agent applies them. If the input sheet instead says to propose one, the agent reads whatever comp documents are sitting in the client's folder and proposes a band, and then nothing finished is produced until she confirms or replaces it. A finished page shows the three multiples and the source and says nothing about who entered them or when.
Two things about the income method, both from Gary's walkthrough. It projects five years of revenue and profitability rather than growing a single earnings figure, and the discount rate is built up from its parts and printed on the page. Peer Comps' version averages six risk scores and calls it a buildup, which is what Gary was reacting to. Also corrected while rebuilding: the terminal value was not growing the final year one more period before capitalizing. Small, invisible, and it moves the number.
Still waiting on Nancy for the income method inputs, question 12 below. The cash-and-receivables step is held on question 7. Everything else in this station runs today.

05: Boundaries

What the agents will not do. Not limitations to fix later. Design decisions.

Finalizing the multiple, and the weighting
Judgment

The weighting is Nancy's, full stop. A station can source and propose a multiple, but a person still says yes before anything finished is produced. An agent that could finalize it could produce a valuation, and then nobody could say whose opinion it was.

The industry code
Gary's call, September 15

Confirmed by a person, read by the agent. A CPA's code on a tax return is often only close, and that conversation happens internally, not in the machine.

The signature
Accountability

Nothing is client-ready until a person has read the working notes and signed. Nothing leaves the machine on the agents' say-so.

Walking a client through it
The conversation

The reports exist so Gary can sit next to an owner and take them through the process. The agents build the artifact. They don't do the meeting.

One structural safeguard worth naming. When an agent doesn't know something, it stops and asks rather than filling the gap. On a page that means the line is simply left blank, with no invented figure and no label explaining itself to a client who never asked. The question itself goes to Nancy in the working notes she reads before she signs. A visible gap is recoverable. A plausible invention that reaches a client is not.
