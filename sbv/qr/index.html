For advisors who serve owners

Why this matters to your practice

For many business owners the company is the largest asset in their plan, and the least documented, least diversified and hardest to value. When a planner or wealth advisor can talk about it with confidence, the owner sees a trusted advisor who understands the whole picture. And when the business is one day sold, the advisor who helped prepare is best positioned to help manage what comes after.

The checklist is deliberately high level. Use it to ask better questions. When a client needs the detailed work, that is what we do.

The SBV Proven Process

The 18 items and why each matters

Four steps: KNOW the value, PROTECT it, GROW it, REAP it.

Step 01

Know

Understand what the business is worth and what the owner needs.

  1. 01

    Get an independent market valuation

    It might be the most valuable asset, and the most illiquid.

  2. 02

    Define the owner's number: after-tax proceeds needed for retirement

    Selling price can face a substantial tax liability, plan ahead.

  3. 03

    Clarify exit goals and timeline: sale, family, employees or keep

    Each path has different timing, tax and readiness requirements.

  4. 04

    Know what buyers pay more for

    Understand value drivers that command a premium in your industry.

Step 02

Protect

Keep the value from disappearing when something changes.

  1. 05

    Be financially prepared

    Know your red flags before buyers do.

  2. 06

    Understand the hidden risk in your business

    Where are you most vulnerable to business value decline?

  3. 07

    Secure key employees with documented roles, employment agreements and long-term incentives

    Losing one person can drop value overnight.

  4. 08

    Keep buy-sell and ownership agreements current, funded and formula-based

    Outdated agreements cause disputes at death, disability, divorce or partner exit.

  5. 09

    Test owner dependence: could the business run 90 days without the owner?

    Have a plan for all contingencies.

  6. 10

    Two most common value killers: inconsistent revenue growth and personnel

    Take action on these two fundamentals that can add multiples to your selling price.

Step 03

Grow

Improve the drivers that raise value.

  1. 11

    Develop a growth plan that accelerates business value

    Align strategy and resource allocation with the growth that matters most.

  2. 12

    Connect key performance indicators (KPIs) to employee compensation plans

    Aligned incentives turn strategy into daily behavior.

  3. 13

    Manage expenses with vigilance

    Target consistent gross profit margins and scrutinize operating expenses.

  4. 14

    Measure what you manage

    Produce accurate month-end financials and daily, weekly and monthly KPIs for all stakeholders.

Step 04

Reap

Plan for an exit with no regrets.

  1. 15

    Evaluate your plan every six months

    Assess personal, business and financial goals, and any changes.

  2. 16

    Line up your deal team early

    Include a valuation partner, M&A advisor and experienced deal attorney.

  3. 17

    Address personal transition matters

    Allow sufficient time to stay on after the sale, up to a year if necessary.

  4. 18

    Align financial, personal and business readiness

    When all three are ready, you have the freedom to choose your next step.
