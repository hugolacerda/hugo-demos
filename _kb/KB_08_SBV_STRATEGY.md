## Core Values

Gary wants to reorder these -- Honest Approach is his #1 despite being listed last.

1. **Clarity Over Complexity** -- Simplify what others overcomplicate so owners can act
   with confidence. Plain language, no jargon, get to the point. The anti-example Gary
   used: a 60-page valuation report from a competitor that was full of filler. SBV's
   reports should be clear, not comprehensive for its own sake.

2. **Education First** -- Teaching precedes advising. Informed owners make better
   decisions. Gary's analogy: give a man a fish vs. teach him to fish. The goal is
   not to tell owners what to do -- it's to equip them so they can make better decisions
   themselves, especially operational decisions that have value implications (e.g., buying
   a truck vs. investing in marketing that drives enterprise value).

3. **Ownership Mindset** -- Businesses are assets to be managed, not just operations to
   run. Gary wants owners to start thinking like investors in their own businesses.

4. **Honest Approach** -- Speak plainly and truthfully about value, risk, and options.
   Gary considers this the most important value. SBV will not sugarcoat valuations or
   tell owners what they want to hear. Internally this also means honest conversations
   about what's working and what isn't.

5. **Extreme Ownership** (working title) -- Concept from Jocko Willink. The idea as Gary
   interprets it for SBV: "We will teach you but you own it." SBV equips the owner; the
   owner is responsible for what they do with that knowledge. Gary may change the label
   but the concept stays. Related: SBV wants clients who will do the work, not passive
   recipients of reports.

## Three Uniques

These differentiate SBV from every other valuation firm:

1. **We bridge the gap** -- between where the owner is today (running the business,
   thinking about how to get out) and the future state they want. Most advisors are
   selling something. SBV is bridging a mindset gap and empowering the owner.

2. **We teach owners to think like investors** -- connecting value-building to the
   operational decisions they're already making. The goal is not to ask them to do
   anything different -- it's to think about what they already do through a value lens.
   Investors think about value, growth, tax mitigation, and professional support. SBV
   helps owners think the same way.

3. **We are building the first AI-powered value optimization firm** -- embedding
   intelligence across insight, workflow, and decision-making. This is the technology
   angle Hugo directly supports.

## Proven Process

> **Know the Value >> Protect the Value >> Grow the Value >> Reap the Reward**

This is the client-facing process framework. It frames SBV's engagement as a progression
rather than a one-time valuation report. Gary's goal is for clients to check in annually
at minimum, with higher-touch clients engaging 2x per year or more.

**Annual engagement model:** Sit with the client in October for planning built around
Value Builder numbers, geared toward value acceleration. Check in ~6 months later for
the annual valuation update. This is the rhythm Gary wants to systematize.

## Target Market

**Primary:** Founder-led, privately held businesses generally under $20M in revenue,
including owners planning to transition in the next 1-5 years and the advisors who
support them.

**Important clarification:** The 1-5 year window does not mean retirement. Many younger
owners -- especially in service businesses and marketing companies -- want to exit well
before retirement age to do something else. SBV should not frame its messaging around
retirement.

**Secondary / referral channel:** Wealth advisors who manage business owner clients.
Gary's insight from the March 24 meeting: wealth advisors with business owner clients
are often scared to discuss the business itself and its exit because they fear losing
the relationship. They feel they don't know enough. They take the CEPA certification
to speak more intelligently. But they still don't do the valuation and exit work --
that's where SBV fits alongside them as the specialist.

## The Marla Model

Gary references Marla (Raincatcher brokerage) as the north star for SBV's lead
generation strategy. Around 2012-2013, Marla was generating 400 leads per month from
cost-efficient digital marketing, converting 70% to $3,000 business readiness valuations.
She eventually built the largest brokerage firm in the country, sold to her partners,
then built a second successful brokerage.

**Gary's lead funnel math (modeled on Marla):**

| Stage | Number | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Annual valuation goal | 100 | Gary's budget target (Nancy wants 200) |
| From Facebook inbound | 25 | 25% of 100 |
| Converting to consulting | 10 | 40% of 25 |

The goal is a system that generates leads at scale, converts them to valuations, then
converts a portion to ongoing advisory relationships. The system matters as much as the
revenue it generates -- a repeatable lead engine is what creates enterprise value in SBV.

## Key Insight: Why Business Owners Act Irrationally

Gary described this at length in the March 24 meeting. Understanding it is essential
context for how SBV positions its education-first approach:

Business owners are often coached by CPAs to spend money on depreciating assets (trucks,
equipment) to reduce taxable income. The CPA optimizes for taxes. Nobody optimizes for
enterprise value. Gary's example: an owner spent $600K putting money into a defined
benefit plan and bought a company truck to get the tax write-off. Two years later the
truck was worth $55K with an $80K loan on it, and the business was declining. The
tax savings were real but the enterprise value impact was never factored in.

SBV's education mission is specifically about this: helping owners understand that every
operational decision has a value implication. They don't need to change what they do --
they need to think about it differently.

**Application for ADS agents and tools:** Educational tools that surface this tension
(operational decision vs. value implication) will resonate strongly with the target
audience. The Net Proceeds Calculator is one example -- showing the gap between gross
sale price and actual net proceeds is exactly this kind of value-clarifying tool.
