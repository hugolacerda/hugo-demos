## Overview

Value Builder is a platform used by business advisors -- brokers, CPAs, financial advisors, coaches -- to run business readiness assessments and manage client nurture. Gary and Nancy use it as their primary CRM across GBA and SBV. The platform includes the Freedom Score assessment, Smart Flows for automated nurture, and a content library of weekly podcast episodes, monthly articles, and quarterly webinars.

The Funnel Builder Boot Camp (March 24, 2026) was run by Kimberly Kirby, Value Builder's training lead. It was grounded in top quartile research across nearly 900 advisors and covered four sections: Sharpen Your Aim, The End Game Conversation, Entrepreneurial Biorhythms, and The Trust Multiplier.

---

## Top Quartile Advisor Behavior

Value Builder defines top quartile advisors as those running the highest number of assessments annually. The research found consistent patterns regardless of business model:

- **Top advisors do not lead with the platform.** They lead with their own expertise (M&A, wealth planning, tax, coaching) and use Value Builder to support that structure and conversation. When the platform is positioned as the strategy itself rather than a tool within a broader strategy, it stalls.
- **Top advisors introduce assessments early**, treating them as a catalyst for deeper conversations and better conversion rates -- not as a nice-to-have later in the process.
- **Top advisors use all three assessments** more consistently rather than picking one based on the situation.
- **Top advisors focus on exit-focused owners.** About 1 in 3 top quartile advisors focus specifically on exit-oriented owners, versus 1 in 5 average advisors.

**Application for SBV:** Gary's education-first approach and early assessment introduction already mirrors top quartile behavior. The opportunity is making that pattern more systematic and scalable.

## Market Segmentation -- The Zebra Framework

Value Builder segments the business market into three size bands by revenue. This framework directly shapes how advisors should position and where they should spend their time.

| Segment | Revenue Range | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Chipmunks** | Under $1M | High energy, tight budgets, heavy education lift, smaller fees |
| **Zebras** | $1M -- $10M | Sweet spot. Big enough to pay, small enough to care. Owner-led, motivated to act. |
| **Whales** | $20M+ | Larger teams, more complexity, longer sales cycles, multiple stakeholders |

### Why Zebras Are the Sweet Spot

Two forces intersect in the $5M--$10M range:

- **Appetite for strategic advice** is highest at smaller revenue levels and decreases as companies grow (the owner becomes less involved in day-to-day decisions)
- **Ability to pay for strategic advice** is lowest at smaller revenue levels and increases as companies grow

The sweet spot -- "big enough to pay, small enough to care" -- sits at the crossover point around $5M--$10M in revenue.

Additional data from Value Builder's research:
- Zebras are overrepresented in assessment completion data relative to their share of the overall market
- Zebras are 25% more likely to have already received a written offer to buy their business
- Businesses in the $1M--$10M range are still directly owner-led, which means the owner has both the motivation and the authority to act

**Application for SBV:** SBV's stated target of under $20M revenue is well-aligned with the zebra framework. The messaging for a $5M owner and a $15M owner should differ -- see the $5MM vs $50MM framing below.

## The $5MM Owner vs. $50MM CEO

Business size changes how you need to show up in a conversation. Same industry, same exit timeline -- very different engagement approach.

| | $5MM Owner | $50MM CEO |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | I am the business | I lead the business |
| Motivated by | Freedom, control | Scale, performance |
| Decision style | Emotion and trust | Data and ROI |
| Communication | Conversational, story-based | Concise, insight-driven |
| Values | Simplicity | Strategy and measurable impact |
| Your framing | Problem Solver: "Let's make the business work for you" | Strategic Partner: "Let's maximize value and growth efficiency" |

## Industry Focus

Top quartile advisors are significantly more likely to specialize in a specific industry. Business owners consistently report wanting someone who understands their world -- the language, the pressures, the daily challenges -- without needing explanation. Trust builds faster when the advisor speaks the same language from the first conversation.

**Case example -- Jennifer Ramos:** Former business owner, broker, CEPA. Focused exclusively on the home health and home care industry. Her advice: niche down to what you know, go deep on it, double down. Once she stopped speaking broadly, conversations became easier and people leaned in. Her business is now approximately 90% advisory. She uses Value Builder content as a starting point -- sometimes posting as-is, sometimes connecting a specific line to a known industry pain point.

**Key observation:** Referrals become easier when your role is obvious. When people know exactly who you help and what you do, they know when to think of you.

**Note on GBA:** Gary has explicitly chosen not to position GBA as an industry specialist -- GBA is intentionally generalist, which is why the Industry Insider ADS agent was built as a dynamic tool rather than static industry pages. This is a deliberate exception to the industry-focus principle and does not conflict with it.

## The Three Psychographic Profiles

This is the most directly actionable section for ADS agent design and marketing messaging. Owners are not uniform -- they have different motivations, different relationships with exit, and different trust patterns. Top advisors adjust their approach based on which profile they are engaging.

### Profile 1 -- Mountain Climbers

**Core motivation:** Achievement and growth. Selling the business is the trophy -- the ultimate goal. When they reach the top of one mountain, they immediately look for the next higher peak.

**Money mindset:** Money is a scoreboard. They often have a specific number in mind and want to know where they stand relative to peers. They like to win the negotiation.

**Equity:** Open to sharing equity if it accelerates growth and gets them to the goal faster.

**Exit relationship:** Not squeamish. Often see selling as a rite of passage.

**Trust signals:** Results, benchmarks, comparative performance, what winning looks like.

**How to engage:** Lead with outcomes, performance, and achievement language. Show them where they stand. Make the exit feel like an earned milestone.

---

### Profile 2 -- Freedom Fighters

**Core motivation:** Financial freedom and independence. They did not start a business to build an empire -- they started it because they did not want a boss.

**Career pattern:** Often one company for a very long time. Titles like President or Owner. Deeply tied to the business, may describe it like a family.

**Money mindset:** Money represents freedom, not winning. The business is their retirement plan. It is about having enough to do what they want when they want.

**Equity:** Resistant to sharing. Independence is the whole reason they started the business. Answering to investors or a board goes against who they are.

**Exit relationship:** Complicated. If asked about selling, they will say something like "I guess I'll retire someday." They need a gentle nudge to start thinking about it today. Their biggest concern is what comes next -- selling can create anxiety if they are not ready to stop working.

**Trust signals:** Long-standing relationships. They will default to their accountant, lawyer, and advisors they have known for years. Industry expertise matters enormously to this group -- they have often spent decades in their industry and they are the HVAC company, not just the owner of one.

**Tools:** Freedom Score and Freedom Point are highly motivating for this profile.

**How to engage:** Lead with freedom and what the business enables. Acknowledge the anxiety around what comes next early in the conversation. Do not push the exit conversation too hard too fast. Position yourself as someone who helps them get more out of what they already have.

**Relevance to CEPA outreach:** Many of the business owners wealth advisors manage are Freedom Fighters. The CEPA messaging should reflect this -- SBV helps their clients have the end-game conversation in a way that feels natural and specifically addresses the "what comes next" anxiety without forcing a decision.

---

### Profile 3 -- Craftspeople

**Core motivation:** Mastery. They care deeply about how the work is done. The highest compliment you can give a craftsperson is about the quality of what they have created.

**Career pattern:** Often a tapestry of employment and self-employment across different economic cycles. Risk-averse. Most common outcome: fully owner-operated, not structured for equity sharing.

**Money mindset:** Price-sensitive. Often blur personal and business finances.

**Exit relationship:** Often believe selling is something bigger companies do. When they exit, they are more likely to close the doors or return to employment.

**Biggest fear:** Not valuation -- losing their sense of purpose.

**Trust signals:** Consumer-like behavior. May take advice from almost anyone, including a well-intentioned brother-in-law.

**How to engage:** Compliment the craft, not the financials. Acknowledge their expertise. Be aware that this profile is often not an ideal valuation or exit planning client -- they are lower priority for SBV's target audience.

## The End Game Conversation

According to the Exit Planning Institute, approximately 75% of business owners would like to exit their business within the next 10 years. This is not a niche topic -- it is already on the owner's mind. It surfaces in moments: after a strong sales month, after a tough one, when they miss a family event, when their partner asks how much longer they are going to do this.

Once an owner decides they want to exit (vs. just thinking about it), they invest nearly 60% more in outside advice. The shift from curious to committed changes everything about what they need and how much they will invest.

Top quartile advisors lean into the exit conversation. Most average advisors avoid it because it feels too early, too sensitive, or too risky -- so they stay in the safe territory of growth, revenue, and optimization. Top performers do the opposite, but they do it in a way that feels natural and not forced.

**The advisor who owns the end-game conversation has a greater chance of owning the relationship.**

## The "Define Your Role" Framework

Value Builder presented a simple positioning template for advisors to clarify their focus:

> **"I help \\_\\_\\_ who are \\_\\_\\_ so they can \\_\\_\\_."**

Example from the session: *"I help manufacturing owners who are starting to think about selling -- but aren't sure what their business is worth -- so they can plan their exit with confidence instead of leaving money on the table."*

**Draft versions for SBV:**

- "I help founder-led businesses under $20M who want to understand the value of what they've built so they can make smarter operational decisions and eventually transition on their own timeline."
- "I help Florida business owners who are thinking about their exit -- but aren't sure when or what their business is worth -- so they can know where they stand before anyone else does."

These are worth refining with Gary and testing in CEPA outreach and LinkedIn content.

## Terminology

- **Freedom Score** -- Value Builder's business readiness assessment. Measures how sellable and value-optimized a business is. Gary and Nancy use it as the entry point for SBV engagements.
- **Freedom Point** -- A threshold concept for Freedom Fighters: the point at which the business generates enough value to fund the owner's desired lifestyle. Highly motivating for this psychographic.
- **Top Quartile Advisors** -- Value Builder's designation for the advisors running the highest volume of assessments annually. Used as the benchmark for best practice behavior in all their training.
- **Zebras** -- Value Builder's term for businesses in the $1M--$10M revenue range. Named because they are "hiding in plain sight" -- overlooked by advisors chasing whales, but the most engaged and action-oriented segment.
- **Whales** -- Value Builder's term for $20M+ businesses. Higher fees but longer sales cycles and more complexity.
- **Chipmunks** -- Value Builder's term for sub-$1M businesses. High energy but tight budgets and heavy education lift.
- **Smart Flows** -- Automated nurture sequences inside Value Builder. Gary wants to migrate from Amanda's manual contact management to Smart Flows for re-engagement and ongoing nurture.
- **Built to Sell Radio** -- Weekly podcast produced by Value Builder. Part of the standard nurture content cycle Gary's contacts receive.
- **Kimberly** -- Value Builder's consulting contact for GBA/SBV. The team's go-to for best practice questions on the platform.
